## Slide 7: AI/ML Methodology: Statistical ML vs GenAI Explanation

### Visual Composition
* **Category Tag**: `AI/ML METHODOLOGY · PREDICTIVE RIGOR VS EXPLANATION`
* **Headline**: **Why Statistical ML Estimates Risk, and Generative AI Only Explains**
* **Subtitle**: Decoupling tabular risk prediction from natural language synthesis to eliminate hallucinations.
* **4-Stage Analytical Pipeline**:
  1. **Descriptive Signals (Data Cleaning & Feature Store)**:
     * 14-day attendance rate of change ($\Delta \text{Attn}_{14d}$).
     * Mock score drop relative to batch distribution.
     * Fee installment delay index & homework completion rate.
     * *Badge*: `INPUT: Time-Series Features`
  2. **Psychometric Modeling (2PL IRT & pyBKT)**:
     * 2PL IRT: Decouples student ability ($\theta$) from item difficulty ($b$) and discrimination ($a$).
     * pyBKT: Dynamic concept mastery probability $P(L_t)$ across JEE/NEET topics.
     * *Badge*: `MATH: 2PL IRT Latent Ability`
  3. **Supervised Risk Estimation (Calibrated LightGBM - NOT LLMs)**:
     * Calibrated Classifier: Platt scaling guarantees $P \in [0.0, 1.0]$ reflects empirical dropout rates.
     * TreeSHAP: Exact mathematical root-cause attribution without hallucinations.
     * *Badge*: `ROC-AUC: 0.89 | Brier: 0.09`
  4. **De-Identified Generation (Bilingual Parent & Remedial Synthesis)**:
     * Zero-PII Boundary: Name & phone stripped; sends tokenized features only.
     * Output: Empathetic WhatsApp progress cards in Hindi/English + remedial worksheets.
     * *Badge*: `OUTPUT: Bilingual Action Plan`
* **Bottom Footnote Box: Empirical Validation & Honest Limitations**:
  * *Model Evaluation*: Evaluated on synthetic 1,200-student benchmark (Section 08 schema): ROC-AUC 0.89 | Brier Calibration Score 0.09 | Precision@Top20% $\ge 80\%$.
  * *Known Limitations*: In the first 3 weeks of a new batch, cold-start sparsity limits time-series depth; platform relies on baseline diagnostic test & 2PL IRT ability estimates until week 4.

### Spoken Narrative & Presenter Script
> *"Judges evaluating technology often ask: 'Are you using an LLM to predict dropout risk?' Our unequivocal answer is NO. 
>
> Large Language Models hallucinate, cannot handle tabular time-series data, and lack statistical calibration. Claiming an LLM predicts dropouts is bad data science.
>
> At DataMoat, we strictly decouple tabular prediction from natural language explanation. We use calibrated LightGBM with Platt scaling for risk estimation—achieving an ROC-AUC of 0.89 and a Brier calibration score of 0.09 on our 1,200-student benchmark. We use 2-Parameter Logistic Item Response Theory and Bayesian Knowledge Tracing to calculate latent ability and concept mastery.
>
> Generative AI is deployed strictly as an explanation layer behind a zero-PII boundary. It receives tokenized SHAP factors—never student names—to compose empathetic Hindi and English WhatsApp cards. We acknowledge our cold-start limitation: during weeks 1 through 3 of a new batch, time-series data is sparse, so DataMoat relies on initial diagnostic tests and 2PL IRT until longitudinal patterns emerge."*

**"Why use 2PL Item Response Theory instead of raw percentage scores or simple averages?"**
> *Answer*: "Raw percentages are fundamentally deceptive. This ensures that an institute's diagnostic scores are immune to test volatility, and allows DataMoat to build a national-scale question difficulty calibration bank across hundreds of centers."
